Example event listings.

These listings are examples of how saying I went could work. Each programme is published as open calendar records, and anyone who went can say so in their own account, pointing at the event. The organisers don't publish this way, so we compiled the listings ourselves from their public announcements. The real version would be the organiser's own: the BFI, for example, could put an I went button beside each film on its festival site.

Run one of these programmes?

The better version of any listing here is your own. Publish your programme in your own account, in the shared calendar format, and add an I went button beside each event on your own site. People who press it keep the record in their own account, and it points straight at your event. You don't need our permission or our tools.

If something here is wrong, or you'd rather it wasn't listed, get in touch and we'll correct or remove it.

What these examples show

They show how I went works end to end: a programme published as open records, a button beside each event, and a record written to the visitor's own account that points back at the event.

They don't show numbers. Only a handful of records have been made, almost all for the London Film Festival, by two of us trying it out. Treat any counts on these pages as a check that the plumbing works, not as results.

We're not adding more listings by hand.

How these listings work

The events are community.lexicon.calendar.event records, the shared calendar format used across the network, not a CultureBlocs one. For these examples they're published from @cultureblocs.com. Each page reads the records live, so a correction appears without a site update.

When you press I went, the record is written to your account and points at the event. Pages here find those records through Constellation, an open index of the network run by a third party, and then read each record from its author's account. CultureBlocs doesn't run an index of its own. Delete your record and it disappears from the page.
