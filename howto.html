How to.

Recipes for publishing and reading CultureBlocs records. The first four need only a browser and an account on the network. The rest are for people running their own tools.

publish your programme · say "I went" · claim a work · put it on a site · write a bead · tell a strand · keep a proposal · publish a strand · bring a record home

What you need first

An AT Protocol account. A Bluesky handle works; so does Blacksky, Eurosky, or a server you run. That's all the browser tools need.

For your own tools: the command-line recipes below assume a String at http://localhost:8100. Set STRING_URL and STRING_TOKEN if yours is elsewhere.

docker compose up -d --build # in cultureblocs-string → :8100 the String, :8101 timeline docker compose up -d # in cultureblocs-loom → :8108 Loom

In a browser

Publish your programme

Your programme is published as a shared record that no one owns: community.lexicon.calendar.event, maintained by Lexicon Community. Any calendar or listings app that reads it can show your events, not only ours. Publish under your organisation's own account; that is what makes it your programme rather than someone's copy.

In a browser

Open Publish an event, sign in with your organisation's account and fill in the form. Add a venue profile once, with your address and access information.

From your box office

If your programme already lives in Ticket Tailor or a calendar feed, it can be published from there instead of retyped. That's how the Peckham Digital and Games Transformed listings were built. Get in touch if you'd like to try it.

On the command line

# once: the venue as a publishing party, at rkey "self" python scripts/venue.py profile --name "The Ivy House" --address "40 Stuart Rd" \ --capacity 150 --accessibility "step-free entry, accessible toilet" \ --link "https://…|website" # a night, in the shared type python scripts/venue.py event --title "Friday Session" \ --start 2026-10-02T20:00:00Z --end 2026-10-02T23:00:00Z \ --city London --venue-name "The Ivy House" --link "https://dice.fm/…|tickets" # optional: who is on python scripts/venue.py lineup --event-uri at://…/community.lexicon.calendar.event/… \ --event-cid bafy… --billing "The Bug Club|live" python scripts/promote.py publish <record-id> --identity ivyhouse

People who came can then publish their own records pointing at your event, and you can see those public references. You collect nothing about anyone; what you see is what people chose to publish. Early on that will be a few records an event: a good record of a night, but not audience figures, and it shouldn't be presented as if it were.

Say “I went”

A record that you went to something, kept in your own account.

From a listing

Press I went on any event in a listing (I saw for a film). Sign in with your account, check the day, write a line if you like, and publish. The record goes to your account and points at the event. You can also post it to Bluesky.

Afterwards

Correct or delete it in My beads. Gather a week of them into a strand from the listing's “my week” page.

For something that isn't listed

Write a bead yourself (see Write a bead) and paste the event's at:// address, or simply name it in the note.

Claim a work

A work claim tags a piece of work as yours. It lives in your own repository, and that is the claim. What it proves is narrow and honest — this claim was made on this date by the holder of this repository — not authorship, not ownership. That narrowness is the feature. Unattested is the normal state, not a deficient one: there are no verification badges and no completeness scores.

In Easel

Open Easel, sign in with your handle, fill in your profile once, then + New work: title, date, description, credits, links, external identifiers and images with alt text. Publish it to your repository. Easel tracks drift, so a work you have edited since publishing says so, and republishing updates the same record rather than making a second one.

On the command line

python scripts/creative.py profile --name "Your Name" \ --disciplines "sound,installation" --based "London" python scripts/creative.py work --title "Tideline" --date 2024 \ --description "Four-channel piece for a tidal river." \ --credit "Your Name|composer" --link "https://…|listen" \ --external-id isni:0000000121174403 python scripts/promote.py publish <record-id> --identity personal

Layers reference; they never grow the core. Industry metadata, scene vocabularies and licensing terms belong in other namespaces pointing at a work’s URI, which is why the work record stays tiny. Registration with ISNI, ISWC and the rest is somebody else’s business — externalIds is the hook such a bridge writes back into.

Put published records on a site

Everything published is readable by anything that speaks ATProto, including three readers of ours that need no hosting behind them.

The strand embed

A dependency-free web component. Point it at a handle and it reads that repository live; point it at a baked export and the markup is identical.

<script type="module" src="/cultureblocs-strands.js"></script> <cultureblocs-strands actor="cultureblocs.com" limit="5"></cultureblocs-strands>

The wall and the catalogue

The wall is one person’s published cultureblocs, newest first, with a URL per strand: /wall/<handle>/ and /wall/<handle>/<rkey>. The catalogue is an artist’s published works — /catalogue/?actor=<handle>&template=grid, with index and feature as the other templates, or your own same-origin stylesheet via &css=. Both read the actor’s own PDS in the browser, so nothing of ours sits between a reader and the record.

A static export, with photos

python scripts/export_public.py export <strand-id> --out <site>/cultureblocs

Same privacy strip, plus media copied content-addressed.

Running your own tools

For people using the String, Loom or the command line. Devices and prototypes live on the experiments page →

Write a bead

A bead is one moment: a gallery afternoon, an album, a book finished at breakfast, a film, an encounter. It carries a kind, a time, a note, tags, a place, photos, links, and refs — what it is about. Provenance is fixed at the moment you save it and never changes afterwards; the content stays editable, and a correction is recorded as an edit.

In Loom

Open settings, point Loom at your String, check, then import — that pulls what is already there. Press + New bead and fill the one form: kind, when it happened, the note, tags, place, photos, links and refs. Save writes it to this browser; send takes it to the String.

On a phone

Open Pocket, choose a mask, press the button. Beads queue locally and flush to your String, or publish straight to your own repository if you signed in. If you scanned a venue’s QR on the way in, that night’s event is already attached.

From a totem

Press the button on the device; plug it in later. Loom holds the serial port, reads the dump, and resolves the totem’s elapsed-time counters into real instants — the device has no clock and no network by design.

On the command line

python scripts/mint.py --note "Ben Pester, The Expansion Project" --kind read \ --tags "fiction,sf" --place "home" --time 2026-09-18T08:20:00Z

Kinds: bloc, visit, dwell, encounter, read, listen, watch, screening, performance, note. The list is advisory in the schema, so it can grow without breaking old records.

Tell a strand

A strand is the story of a day or an evening, and the unit you publish. It has a title, a day, a narrative of up to 10,000 characters, a place, links, refs — and the beads it strings together. The beads do not change when you add them; the strand points at them.

In Loom

Press + New strand. While it is open, every bead in the String column carries a tick box: ticking one puts it in the strand, in order. Ticking a proposal keeps it at the same time. Write the narrative beside the column, save, and send.

In the timeline

Load a day at :8101, select the beads you want and choose group into strands. The strand gets its own title, place and event link.

Refs are the part worth the effort. Naming the film, the book or the venue as a ref — subject for what the entry is about, mention for what it reaches for — is what lets two people writing about the same night ever find each other. The text itself is never rewritten: no wikilinks, no markup, just a small array beside it. See the shared defs.

Keep or release a proposal

Machines may suggest; only a person decides. The scrobbler clusters Last.fm plays into listening sessions and proposes one listen bead per session; those arrive with state: proposal and sit on a dotted rail. A proposal may be revised by a later run — a set that turned out to have more tracks in it — and a re-run will not erase edits you have made to it. The moment you keep one, it becomes a mint fact and no worker can touch it again. Release discards it.

Both buttons are in the String column in Loom, and on the dotted rail in the timeline. This is the whole contract: proposals are not facts.

Publish a strand

Publishing is a deliberate act, per strand, never a default. The String holds your identities — use app passwords, never account passwords — and multiple identities are the point: personal strands publish as you, organisational strands as the org. Same desk, different letterhead.

curl -X PUT http://localhost:8100/identities/personal \ -H 'content-type: application/json' \ -d '{"handle":"you.bsky.social","appPassword":"xxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx"}' python scripts/promote.py publish <strand-id> --identity personal python scripts/promote.py status # what has drifted since it was published

The timeline has publish, republish and unpublish buttons per strand, with a confirm showing which identity will speak. What publishes: place names, notes, tags, links, kinds, times, and refs. What never leaves: geo coordinates, provenance, device identifiers, mint ids, local media, and any person named only by a name.

That strip is one rule with two implementations — Python in the String, JavaScript in the browser — held together by shared fixtures, so the two cannot quietly disagree about what leaves your machine. It fails closed: a field or a ref type it does not recognise is dropped rather than guessed at.

Bring a born-public record home

A bead minted on a phone and published straight to your repository has no private original — it was born public. Bring it home to annotate it, attach photos and put it in a strand:

python scripts/import_repo.py --actor you.bsky.social --dry-run python scripts/import_repo.py --actor you.bsky.social

Imported records keep their public identity, so re-running imports nothing twice, and they remember their published twin: because the String reuses the original record key, re-publishing updates the public record in place rather than leaving an orphaned duplicate beside it.

Two things follow from being born public. There is no richer private version to fall back on — what you minted is what the world saw. And every later edit is a public amendment, visible in your repository’s history. That is honest, and worth knowing before you edit a note someone has already read.
